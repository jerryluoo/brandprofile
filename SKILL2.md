## What the company appears to be

Inference AI is positioning itself as a full-stack AI infrastructure and enablement company.

Core umbrella idea:
- "Everything inference. One platform."
- One stack, four products.

Those products are framed as:
- Ghost: one-click deployment for persistent AI agents / VMs
- Maestro: smart model routing, budget control, unified bill, cheapest model that meets the need
- Engine: wholesale compute / GPUs from high-end bare metal down to lower-cost options
- Academy: learning-by-building and coaching for AI engineers/builders

This is not just "we sell GPUs."
It is a broader "platform for the AI era" story that combines:
- infrastructure
- orchestration / routing / cost optimization
- agent deployment
- workforce / talent / education

## Voice rules to emulate

When writing in an Inference AI-aligned voice, prefer:
- short, strong statements
- specific outcomes over vague innovation claims
- builders / operators / engineers over generic "users"
- verbs like build, ship, deploy, run, scale, automate, optimize
- language about trust, real-world use, production, accountability, outcomes
- commercially literate framing: cost, ROI, value, budget, efficiency
- confidence without bloated hype
- concrete contrasts: demo vs production, learning vs building, model choice vs smart routing

Sentence behavior:
- often short paragraphs
- punchy headline lines
- occasional contrast structures
- selective use of list formatting for clarity

## Operating brand kit
### Current public message architecture

Use the umbrella only when the post is about the platform as a whole:
- **Umbrella:** Everything inference. One platform.
- **Promise:** Build, deploy, and scale AI without unnecessary infrastructure friction.
- **Proof areas:** agents, model routing, compute, and hands-on education.
- **Emotional payoff:** more people can move from idea to shipped system.

Use one product as the hero in a product post. Do not flatten the four products into interchangeable features.

| Product | Job to be done | Message | Best audience | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ghost | Put an agent into a persistent working environment | Deploy an agent in one click, give it work, and let it run | Agent builders, operators, technical founders | Deploy an agent |
| Maestro | Choose models without manually managing every tradeoff | Route each request by cost, speed, and quality through one API | AI product teams, API users, FinOps-minded operators | Join the waitlist / learn more |
| Engine | Access suitable GPU capacity at the right economics | Bare-metal GPUs and InfiniBand clusters, hourly or reserved | ML teams, researchers, infrastructure buyers | Reserve compute / request pricing |
| Academy | Turn AI knowledge into practical ability | Learn by building on the same stack that runs real systems | Students, early-career engineers, aspiring builders | Join the Academy / join the community |

Status rule: the main site currently labels Maestro “Coming soon.” Avoid copy that implies general availability, pricing, or measured savings unless a current source or user-provided brief confirms it. Engine pricing is shown as “on request”; do not invent rates. Product specifications and capacity claims must be refreshed against the live product page before publishing.

### Audience-specific framing

- **Builder:** “Here is the fastest path to ship this.” Show the workflow, code, deployment, or a real tradeoff.
- **Technical founder:** “Here is how this reduces time and infrastructure overhead.” Tie the product to speed, reliability, and economics.
- **AI/ML operator:** “Here is the control surface.” Discuss routing, budgets, capacity, observability, guardrails, and repeatability.
- **Learner:** “Here is a project you can actually finish.” Make the next step concrete and hands-on.
- **Community member:** “Here is a place to build with other people.” Highlight events, demos, feedback, and shared progress.

### Content pillars and recurring series
Recommended mix: 35% practical builder education, 25% product/workflow proof, 20% industry/operator insight, 15% community and people, 5% direct conversion. Adjust around launches and events.

- **Ship Log:** a build, deployment, demo, or postmortem; show what changed and what was learned.
- **Trade-off Tuesday:** explain one cost/speed/quality or reliability decision in plain language.
- **From Demo to Production:** identify the missing step between a prototype and a dependable workflow.
- **Stack Notes:** concise commentary on GPUs, agents, routing, evaluation, or infrastructure economics.
- **Builder Spotlight:** a person, project, or student outcome; let the builder explain the work.
- **Office Hours / Build With Us:** events, code-alongs, AMAs, Discord activity, and community prompts.
- **Product in Practice:** one product, one use case, one measurable or observable outcome.

A strong post normally follows: **hook → specific problem → concrete mechanism → proof or example → one CTA**. If proof is unavailable, label the post as an opinion, explainer, or preview rather than implying a customer result.

### Platform roles

- **LinkedIn:** primary channel for company positioning, operator insight, launches, hiring, partnerships, and outcome-led explainers. Use a clear thesis, short paragraphs, and 2–4 takeaways. Keep hashtags sparse or omit them.
- **X:** fast channel for product moments, technical observations, launch updates, event energy, and short threads. Put the sharpest point first; lowercase is acceptable for informal community posts.
- **Instagram:** use the Academy/community side most naturally: builder portraits, event recaps, carousels, short demos, and visual explainers. The image or video must carry real information; avoid generic AI imagery.
- **YouTube:** best for build walkthroughs, demos, technical explainers, founder/operator conversations, and Academy lessons. Package each video around the practical outcome.
- **Discord/community:** conversation and participation layer, not a billboard. Ask for builds, feedback, questions, or event participation.

Adapt the expression by platform, but keep the same underlying claim, product status, and proof level. Never copy a LinkedIn post verbatim to every channel.

### Visual and editorial direction

Prefer real product evidence: terminal or dashboard captures, agent workflows, GPU/cluster imagery, diagrams of routing or deployment, project demos, event photography, and people building. Design should feel technical, legible, and high-signal rather than glossy or dystopian. Use restrained motion and clear labels; do not use generic robot art, fake benchmark charts, or visuals that imply capabilities the product does not have.

Use sentence case, short lines, and concrete nouns. Capitalize product names exactly as **Ghost**, **Maestro**, **Engine**, and **Academy**. Avoid unexplained acronyms and dense infrastructure jargon in top-level copy. A good technical post can be precise without sounding like documentation.

### Claim and review guardrails
Before a draft is approved, check:
- Is the product and availability status current?
- Is every number, model name, capacity, speed, or savings claim sourced?
- Does “best,” “cheapest,” “secure,” “reliable,” or “scale” have a defined basis, or should it be softened?
- Does the post distinguish company capability from a customer outcome?
- Is the CTA the correct action for the current page or launch stage?
- Does the post avoid implying autonomous replacement where the intended message is human amplification and accountable execution?

Use source-backed language such as “designed to,” “helps teams,” or “the platform lets builders” when a quantified result is not available. Treat external research and third-party analytics as context, never as proof of Inference AI performance.

### Copy examples

On-brand:
- “A demo proves the idea. Production proves the system.”
- “Pick the model for the job, not the logo on the model card.”
- “Give an agent work. Give it the tools to finish.”
- “Learn AI by building on the stack that runs it.”

Off-brand:
- “The AI revolution is here. Don’t get left behind.”
- “Our revolutionary platform will transform everything.”
- “Replace your team with autonomous intelligence.”

## Default draft/review checklist

Before approving Inference AI social copy, check:

- Brand fit
  - Does it sound builder-first, practical, and outcome-led?
  - Does it avoid empty hype and generic AI slogans?
- Product accuracy
  - Is the right product centered?
  - Does the wording match the current product status?
- Platform fit
  - Is this written for a confirmed platform with the right role?
  - Is the tone adapted rather than copied from another channel?
- Proof level
  - Does every meaningful claim have evidence?
  - Are speculative benefits softened appropriately?
- CTA fit
  - Is the CTA realistic for the current asset and launch stage?
- Audience fit
  - Is the reader a builder, operator, learner, founder, or community member?
  - Does the copy speak to what that audience actually cares about?
